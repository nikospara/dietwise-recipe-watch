Grant Preparation (General Information screen) — Provide an overall description of your project (including context and overall

The DietWise advances the state-of-the-art by proposing systemic changes, a focus on inclusion, and open social
innovations with the aim of developing solutions that streamline existing tools and applications to foster healthy and
sustainable food provision and to make cooking, eating, and treating of food at home the most attractive choice for all
stakeholders. Using disruptive new approaches and voluntary market self-regulation, our activities will help to dampen
nutritional noise gradually and organically merge cultural and commercial practices with a healthier and sustainable food
consumption pattern. Next, we will empower citizens with novel, citizen science-based solutions that will shift the role
citizens play – away from passive actors influenced by the food environment to citizens as active participants influencing
their decisions and helping to create better digital food environments. The project will generate multiple novel insights
to empower citizens to make healthy and sustainable choices and original empirical evidence through 'big data' analysis,
large-scale surveys, qualitative research, and micro-level experiments. From a methodological point of view, we will
harness novel state of the art methods, such as using artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms with deep learning techniques
to analyze big online data. Finally, we will integrate scientific and empirical findings into social innovations powered by
AI and develop user-oriented tools to support citizens. As a result, our methodological and empirical advancements will
provide a deeper understanding of how various external system-level environmental factors shape attitudes and beliefs
towards healthy and sustainable food provision and cooking, how to motivate consumers to follow nutrition guidelines,
how to include the ones who are in greatest need, and how to help citizens shape digital food environments.

# Work package WP4 – Development of the DietWise framework

Work Package Number: WP4
Lead Beneficiary: 1 - KU Leuven
Work Package Name: Development of the DietWise framework
Start Month: 8
End Month: 16

## Objectives

WP4 aims to co-create a framework for the DietWise project in line with the needs of relevant stakeholders, to create a
corresponding capacity-building curriculum, as well as ICT solutions supporting this initiative.

## Description

T4.1. Co-creation of a framework addressing the needs of stakeholder groups – influencers, citizens, and vulnerable
people. (Leader: SAFE; Contributors: PROL, VIGL, IHU, PHB, VU, AdC, KUL, Food).
Task 4.1 involves relevant stakeholders, i.e., influencers, citizens, and vulnerable people, in co-creating the DietWise
concept and its solutions. Building on the insights gained from WP2, we will organize co-creation workshops for each
stakeholder group in every pilot country (three in person/online workshops per country). The objective of the workshops
is to cooperate with different stakeholders to identify needs, barriers and opportunities, with the aim of increasing
compliance with healthy and sustainable nutrition guidelines. To ensure standardization, the coordinators of this task
will design the methodology of co-creation activities. SAFE will lead co-creation workshops for two stakeholder groups:
influencers and citizens, and PROL – for vulnerable citizens. Pilot country teams (PHB, VIGL, IHU) will organize and
facilitate workshops and provide collected information for further framework creation. They will also gather stakeholders
on specified dates and involve them in co-development activities.
T4.2 Co-development of ICT solutions (Leader: ICCS; Contributors: SAFE, PROL, VIGL, IHU, PHB, VU, AdC, KUL,
Food).

Task 4.2 involves close collaboration with selected stakeholder groups in each pilot country to co-develop the best ICT
solutions. After conceptualizing an initial version of the concept in T4.1., we will engage stakeholders in ICT solutions
co-developing processes, such as testing and refining the prototypes of proposed ICTs. The co-development activities
will entail: (i) Co-development for Customization – working in conjunction with stakeholders to co-develop and refine
the ICTs, ensuring they cater specifically to their needs and requirements; (ii) Feature Testing and Evaluation – testing
the effectiveness of key features like weekly planning tools, recipe ratings, as well as advice on waste reduction and
sustainable delivery options. The effectiveness of these features in boosting customer engagement will be thoroughly
assessed, with an emphasis on collecting insightful feedback and implementing continuous enhancements. To ensure
standardization, the framework of co-development activities will be developed by ICCS in cooperation with SAFE – for
influencers and citizens, and PROL – for vulnerable citizens. Pilot country teams (PHB, VIGL, IHU) will be responsible
for gathering stakeholders, organizing, facilitating workshops, and providing collected information for further framework
creation. VU, VIGL, IHU, PROL, PHB will provide advice on how to match ICT solutions with national nutrition
guidelines. The selection criteria from the online platform Zeker Gezond based on the Flemish nutritional guidelines in
the Food triangle can be used as baseline criteria for healthy recipes that comply with national nutritional policies and
guidelines. Usability testing will be performed on prototypes, through user testing platforms like UXtweak. We will test
coded beta apps through platforms like Apple TestFlight or Google Play Console.

T4.3. Capacity building curriculum (Leader: SAFE; Contributors: VU, VIGL, IHU, PROL, PHB).
This task will ensure that capabilities are built and influencers have the possibility to know how to responsibly and
efficiently apply national dietary advice to create recipes and recommendations for eating, treating, and cooking healthy
and sustainable food at home. Such knowledge and understanding of why it is important and beneficial for them will
work as a motivator to join the voluntary initiative RCA, as well as serve as a roadmap for their content and rankings.
Based on the insights from WP2 and WP3, as well as from T4.1-4.2, SAFE will develop a dedicated training programme
to enhance responsible influencing when it comes to healthy and sustainable diets. VU, VIGL, IHU, PROL, PHB will
provide advice on how to match the training programme with national nutrition guidelines. Training content will be (i)
tailored to the beneficiaries’ most relevant aspects, (ii) highly interactive, and (iii) include peer learning or use cases
with hands-on examples and experience to ensure a holistic upskilling experience and that beneficiaries apply their
learned skills in action. Capacity building training on integrity principles and the voluntary reporting principles will
be implemented in T8.2. This curriculum will also be publicly available online. All the intermediate-level stakeholders
who will participate in the voluntary initiative RCA will have the possibility to access this material, making one more
reason to join the initiative.

T4.4. The overall framework of DietWise (Leader: KUL; Contributors: all). We will connect the valuable knowledge
and insights about the needs of citizens and influencers, barriers, and drivers for making changes towards more healthy
and sustainable eating behavior. These insights from WP2, WP3, and WP4 will feed into a holistic framework of
DietWise and supporting solutions for each pilot country. The overall framework will define and explain, e.g., (i)
the mechanism of the voluntary initiative RCA (how it should be managed, organized, implemented, etc.); (ii) the
criteria for the RCA add-on tool and RecipeWatch app; (iii) different participation levels for influencers; (iv) most
efficient behavioral interventions; (v) recommended measures for motivating stakeholders; (vi) context, possible risk
and mitigation measures, applications for adaptations, etc. Integrating findings will fine-tune our approach and provide
valuable insights for strengthening its adaptability and replication potential. We will take into account differences in
our pilot countries, benchmark, and document the potential of different interventions and lessons learned during cocreation activities. In parallel, we will make use of statistical, managerial, and implementation criteria to select the most
efficient interventions.


# Work package WP5 – Development of the DietWise pilot implementation plans

Work Package Number: WP5
Lead Beneficiary: 9 - IHU

Start Month: 13
End Month: 18

## Objectives

WP5 aims to provide a comprehensive overarching methodology, based on SMART goals, adjusted for each pilot country.
WP5 will determine piloting methods including tailored action plans per pilot country, as well as guarantee proper
stakeholder engagement and ICT solutions programmed and ready for the pilots launch in WP7.

## Description

T5.1. The overall methodology of pilot implementation (Leader: KUL; Contributors: all).
Based on the overall framework of DietWise designed in T4.5, this task will develop a structured and detailed
methodology that will serve as a blue-print outlining the scope, objectives, activities, resources, and timelines for the
pilots. Working together with local pilot teams we will develop implementation plans tailored to local needs, challenges,
and national nutritional policies of each pilot country. The plans for each country will provide clear, concise, and easy-touse action plans linking main activities, milestones, timelines, and stakeholder engagement tailored to each pilot country.
The plans will define (a) the list of selected interventions to be implemented in each pilot; (b) respective methodology
for each intervention; (c) any factors that can facilitate or hinder the implementation of interventions, including risk
assessment and mitigation strategies, (d) stakeholder mapping and engagement plans; (e) performance indicators and
assessment criteria. The pilot implementation methodology will also contain a dedicated stakeholder mapping and
engagement plan, in order to facilitate stakeholder engagement and create a strong network that is fundamental for the
success of pilot activities.

T5.2. Developing ICT solutions (Leader: ICCS; Contributors: all).
This task will deliver two ICT solutions, powered by AI and natural language processing (NLP) models. The Responsible
Cooking Alliance (RCA) add-on tool will automatically scrape the content of influencers’ websites/mobile apps and
provide a score representing their compliance with the national nutrition guidelines and promotion of sustainability
practices. Specific criteria for counting the score will be defined during co-creation processes (T4.1). We will use
technologies like Scrapy, Puppeteer, Beautiful Soup, Python Requests for content scraping. Further, we will leverage the
capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the Agile software development approach. Key components of this
task include (a) API Integration – we will strategically utilize the APIs of the respective CMPs for effective data access
and manipulation, ensuring strict adherence to the API usage guidelines established by each platform; (b) Testing for
Compatibility and Functionality – we will implement rigorous testing to confirm the seamless functionality of the addons and plugins across various CMPs, ensuring consistent compatibility across diverse systems.
The RecipeWatch app will be a dynamic, adjustable app that cleverly updates the recommendations to fit user needs,
health, and other nutritional constraints. Each ICT solution will be tailored for deployment in each pilot country, ensuring
alignment with the respective national dietary advice and sustainability practices. Drawing upon the insights garnered
from WP2, WP3, and WP4, we will develop the specifications for ICTs, ensuring that the needs and aspirations of
each stakeholder group are comprehensively addressed and integrated into the framework. This task will progressively
encompass a series of structured development phases, which include: (i) the formulation of detailed specifications (in
collaboration with T4.1), (ii) the establishment of a robust system architecture, (iii) the design and creation of an intuitive
user interface (UI) and user experience (UX), (iv) the deployment and testing of beta version of ICTs (in collaboration
with T4.2).

T5.3. Setting up the pilot in Belgium and stakeholder engagement (Leader: VIGL, Contributors: KUL, Food).
This task will focus on establishing and effectively managing the pilot in Belgium. VIGL, together with KUL, will
develop the plan that will be granularly structured to foresee specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound
(SMART) goals adapted for Belgium, detailed step-by-step breakdowns of activities required to achieve the objectives,
decision-making, and evaluation processes, networking events, and respective timelines and milestones among others,
while clearly outlining roles and responsibilities. The implementation plan will define (a) a list of selected interventions
to implement in each pilot; (b) methods on how these interventions will be integrated into existing apps and tools in
the pilots; (c) identification of factors that can facilitate or hinder the implementation of interventions in each pilot
country. Additionally, it will feature a risk assessment and mitigation strategy that is customized to the local context,
along with a comprehensive plan for engaging stakeholders. We will engage food influencers such as celebrity chefs,
bloggers, nutritionists, and book authors (e.g., Sandra Bekkari, prinsessenboontjes.be; online platform ZekerGezond.be)
to align their meal recipes with nutritional guidelines voluntarily. Beyond implementing the voluntary reporting initiative
RCA and RecipeWatch app, the Belgian pilot will focus on validating behavioral interventions in retail environments
and working with vulnerable children and adolescents (e.g., youth on aid). At the same time, this task will use organic
and promotional pathways to ensure that stakeholders (e.g., citizens, influencers, public authorities, health actors, and
retailers) are involved and ready to participate in pilot activities once pilots are launched in WP7.

T5.4. Setting up the pilot in Greece and stakeholder engagement (Leader: IHU, Contributors: PROL, ICCS).
This task will focus on establishing and managing the pilot in Greece. Similar to T5.3 this task will develop the
implementation plans based on SMART goals adapted for Greece, test various behavioral interventions, and engage
stakeholders. We will engage local food influencers such as celebrity chefs, bloggers, nutritionists, and book authors (e.g.,
Argyro Bargarigou, Akis Petretzikis) to align their meal recipes with national nutrition guidelines. Beyond implementing
the voluntary reporting initiative RCA and RecipeWatch app, the Greek pilot will focus on developing and testing novel
tools for guiding dietary behaviors of vulnerable people (led by PROL). For this, we will target schools that are situated
in low-SES neighborhoods. We will identify and select tools to be tested (such as training school personnel, integrating
nutrition guidelines into the teaching curriculum, nudging packages for schools, etc.). Simultaneously, this task will
focus on efficiently managing interactions with stakeholders in the ecosystem, ranging from citizens and influencers
to public authorities, healthcare entities, and school personnel. Similar to T5.3, stakeholder engagement will capitalize
both on organic and promotional pathways.

T5.5. Setting up the pilot in Lithuania and stakeholder engagement (Leader: PHB, Contributors: AdC, VU).
This task will focus on establishing and effectively managing the pilot in Lithuania. Similar as in T5.3. the task
aims to develop the implementation plans based on SMART goals adapted for Lithuania, validate various behavioral
interventions, and engage stakeholders. We will engage local food influencers such as celebrity chefs, bloggers,
nutritionists (e.g., Nidos receptai, Beatos virtuvė) to align their meal recipes with local nutritional guidelines. Beyond
implementing the voluntary reporting initiative RCA and RecipeWatch app, the Lithuanian pilot will focus on addressing
the needs of vulnerable citizens: elderly and adolescents with eating disorders via cooking classes focused on cooking
healthy and affordable meals (using RecipeWatch and the RCA ratings). Similar to T5.3 the stakeholder engagement
will capitalize on organic and promotional pathways.


# 1. EXCELLENCE

## 1.1. Objectives and ambition

### 1.1.1 Context and innovative approach

Citizens are living in food environments that foster unhealthy and unsustainable food consumption. Deeply
rooted norms and cultural practices, individual habits, and commercial activities act in concert to further reinforce
this situation. There are growing societal and economic concerns that the diet of European citizens needs to become
more healthy and sustainable[^1][^2]. Various influencing agents attempt to address this concern, but governments,
doctors, sports coaches, and influencers tend to produce contradictory dietary advice. To make things worse, the
available scientific evidence is mixed and not always represented in the most accurate way to citizens. This
widespread nutritional noise confuses citizens about healthy and sustainable food consumption and triggers
mistrust in national nutrition guidelines. Healthy and sustainable food consumption advice also often ignores the
specific needs of vulnerable citizens.

We conclude that current advice practices undermine the motivation to engage in healthy and sustainable food
consumption, thereby paradoxically consolidating the status quo. DietWise seeks to address these challenges by
means of disruptive new approaches that apply existing scientific methods in novel ways. Central in our approach
are systemic changes, a focus on inclusion, and open social innovations with the aim of developing solutions
that streamline existing tools and applications (instead of creating new solutions) to foster healthy and sustainable
food provision and to make cooking, eating, and treating of food at home the most attractive choice for all
stakeholders.

[^1]: European Economic and Social Committee (2019). Promoting healthy and sustainable diets in the EU (own-initiative opinion).
[^2]: FAO & WHO (2019). Sustainable healthy diets: Guiding principles. Rome

Systemic solutions to break through nutritional noise are needed because having yet another one or two
government-funded apps or tools will not change the fundamental problem that current food environments foster
unhealthy and unsustainable consumption. Governments do not have powerful enough tools to break through the
hyperabundance of nutrition apps and tools and overcome nutritional noise. Moreover, governments will never be
on par with private entities when it comes to the promotion of (un)healthy and (un)sustainable products, advertising
budgets, and marketing knowledge. For that reason, DietWise proposes to shift towards shared responsibility for
the enhancement of healthy and sustainable diets aligned with national dietary advice from governments toward
food environment actors – who serve as gatekeepers for citizen decision-making. The core idea of our project is
focused on creating a voluntary reporting system assessing and guiding how much each existing app, each
celebrity chef, each food blog, or each retailer meets national dietary advice. Thus, instead of top-down directives
we will explore how to use a bottom-up approach and place the initiative to change dietary behavior directly in the
hands of food environment actors. We believe such a shift will help gradually dampen nutritional noise and
organically merge cultural and commercial practices with a healthier and sustainable food consumption pattern
– resulting in a broad reach and impact.

Next, DietWise advances the state-of-the-art by proposing a more precise and targeted approach – empowerment
through personalization and inclusion. Using the underlying logic of motivational matching – the design and
distribution of persuasive messages such that message features align with the motivational characteristics of
citizens, we will develop a number of social innovations to guide and improve citizen decision-making. Such an
approach will also enable us to address resistance to health and sustainability advice and tailor our interventions to
the heterogeneity in the individual needs of vulnerable citizens. By acknowledging diversity in the consumer base,
we will be capable of helping citizens navigate through the food environment fraught with nutritional noise.
To sustain and scale the change, we will develop policy recommendations and integrity decision-making tool for
the industry (e.g., Where are the limits for promoting unhealthy, unsustainable recipes? How should industry actors
consider sustainability aspects?). We will leverage the interdisciplinary and transdisciplinary competencies of
the consortium in marketing, psychology, nutrition, information sciences, public administration, consumer rights,
and retailing. The project will generate multiple novel solutions to empower citizens to make healthy and
sustainable choices and original empirical evidence through 'big data' analysis, large-scale surveys, qualitative data,
and micro-level experiments. From a methodological point of view, we will harness novel state-of-the-art methods,
such as using artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms with deep learning techniques to analyze big online data.
Finally, we will integrate scientific and empirical findings into social innovations powered by AI and develop useroriented tools to support citizens. As a result, our methodological and empirical advancements will provide a
deeper understanding of how various external system-level environmental factors shape attitudes and beliefs
towards healthy and sustainable food provision and cooking, how to motivate citizens to follow nutrition
guidelines, how to include the ones who are in greatest need, and how to help citizens shape digital food
environments.

### 1.1.2 Project objectives (O)

O1. Understanding food environments and citizen needs. 71 percent of people have "gone digital" in the
kitchen, getting their cooking ideas from Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok and following foodie influencers on
social media [^3]. Yet, only a few studies specifically targeted the link between food environments, cooking at home,
and compliance with nutrition guidelines. Therefore, we will start our project by deepening our understanding of (i)
how food environments shape the diverse needs and motivations of citizens; (ii) what drives the motivation of food
environment actors, specifically influencers, to align their activities with nutrition guidelines; and (iii) how to
address the needs of vulnerable people. We will provide institutional-level insights about existing nutrition
guideline promotion systems, processes, tools, and applications. We will complement this analysis with two large
surveys to obtain a deeper understanding of needs, barriers, and drivers influencing citizens' dietary choices and
determine the current state of engagement with apps/tools/nutrition guidelines among vulnerable people. In
addition, in-depth qualitative research will provide knowledge on how to boost the motivation of (online) food
environment actors (influencers) to align their actions with nutrition guidelines. Relevance to the topic:
‘empowerment of citizens and their capacity to eat and cook at home in line with budgetary and time constraints as
well as their living situation’ and ‘enhanced uptake of beneficial tools and applications by citizens, especially those
who need it most, considering socio-economic characteristics and differences across EU and Associated countries.’

Related WPs, Deliverables, Milestones: WP2 (D2.1-2.4) [M4]. Key KPIs: KPI-1: EU-wide survey: ≥N4000; KPI3: Understanding the motivation of influencers: 3-7 interviews per pilot, KPI-5: Vulnerable citizens survey:
≥N300s.

We define influencers as powerful food environment actors who use wide (primarily digital) media presence to
influence citizens’ food provision and cooking decisions – celebrity chefs, social media influencers, cooking
websites, blogs and apps, retailers providing cooking advice, celebrity nutritionists, and doctors.

[^3]: Brimble, L. (2020, October 28). More than 70% of adults use social media for recipes instead of cookbooks, survey finds. Independent.

O2. Boosting motivation to use nutrition guidelines for cooking with novel behavioral interventions.
Traditional approaches are not powerful enough when it comes to actually changing the target behavior because
they address citizens without considering intrinsic needs (like simply communicating that nutrition guidelines are
‘good for you’). To counteract this, we will develop novel behavioral interventions based on motivational matching
– the design and distribution of persuasive messages such that message features (e.g., the themes emphasized) align
with motivational characteristics of citizens (e.g., salient goals, needs, values) [^4]. To boost citizen uptake of nutrition
guidelines, we will test two large groups of motivationally matched interventions: (1) norms and (2) social
influence. We will align interventions and personal consumer needs with nutrition guidelines by accounting for the
(moderating) roles of nationality, religion, culture, regionality, seasonality, time and financial constraints, waste
reduction, and delivery choices. Relevance to the topic: ‘...enhanced uptake of beneficial tools and applications by
citizens’, ‘include in approaches ‘culinary culture dimension’ <…> and time and financial constraints’, ‘link
solutions to the issue of food waste…’, ‘take a holistic approach, e.g., delivery <….>, short supply chains,
marketing, ….’.

Related WPs, Deliverables, Milestones: WP3 (D3.1.-3.4) [M5]. Key KPIs: KPI-7: Developed and tested innovative
behavioral interventions: at least 3; KPI-9: Demonstration of improved motivation to cook in a sustainable and
healthy way: 5-15% or more; KPI-22: Demonstration of compliance with ‘corrections’ of recipes: 15-30%.

O3. Digital social innovations and AI-based apps to empower citizens. Novel systemic solutions are needed to
counteract the overly unhealthy and unsustainable food environments. Using advances in information science,
emerging ICT technologies, and AI, we will develop and launch two innovative solutions that will integrate and
streamline existing food environment elements that are already used by citizens (e.g., recipe websites, cooking
apps, blogs, influencers' actions). Specifically, the Responsible Cooking Alliance (RCA) is a voluntary
reporting initiative that will guide influencers by assessing how much their actions meet national dietary advice
(e.g., do they promote healthier and more sustainable choices, provide weekly meal planning, promote efforts to
reduce waste?). The AI-powered app RecipeWatch will scan the content of existing recipe websites and apps and
suggest personalized corrections in line with nutrition guidelines (e.g., 'reduce the amount of red meat this week').
These solutions will serve as a platform of social innovation to guide and improve both food environments and
consumer decision-making – empowering citizens to easily choose the most beneficial options. Relevance to the
topic: ‘Creating smart and attractive tools to enhance healthy and sustainable food provision, eating and treating of

O4. Empowering vulnerable people. Unsustainable and unhealthy food environments tend to affect
disproportionally more vulnerable people – low-income, low-education citizens, elderly, children and adolescents,

and youth with eating disorders (Lithuania). We will start the project by defining the most vulnerable citizens in the

deeper into their needs by conducting a large survey targeting vulnerable citizens and aiming to gather in-depth
knowledge of their preferences, food literacy, and behaviors. Finally, we will follow an inclusive approach and
evaluate the accessibility of tools, apps, and behavioral interventions’ for vulnerable people using the 8Bs

cook at home in line with budgetary and time constraints as well as their living situation’ and ‘enhanced uptake of

consumer organizations, and policymakers). We will engage stakeholders at all levels of tools and applications
development to boost novel approaches and innovations – from the joint generation of initial ideas about the

Ambition 1: Linking nutrition guidelines with cooking advice

People in the Northern, Southern, and Western European regions lead the world in preparing home-cooked
meals, with an average of 7.8 meals per week [^6] - indicating that we cook a lot, but the healthiness and
sustainability of cooking is less known.

State of the art. Even though multiple studies show the basic associations between cooking at home and healthier
diets [^7], the picture is more complicated than that. Some studies find that home food preparation is not a prerequisite
to a high-quality diet and that citizens who cook less frequently tend to consume more fruit, low-fat dairy products,
and less red meat [^8]. Moreover, home cooking frequently includes the use of processed ingredients, which is
associated with adverse health outcomes, including weight gain [^9]. These findings nuance the overall positive
association between home cooking on the one hand and healthy and sustainable outcomes on the other hand. In
addition, most prior work linking healthiness and cooking at home is correlational and thus remains speculative
about causality. Is home cooking leading to a healthy diet, or do health-oriented people cook more at home or do
underlying factors such as available time, intrinsic motivation to cook, and budgetary constraints cause or suppress
both independently? Given that the state of the art provides no clear answers to the above-raised questions, it is
essential to understand why and under what conditions cooking at home leads to high dietary quality. We assume
that the more cooking at home is aligned with nutrition guidelines (as representing the best practices on health and
sustainability), the better the outcomes on public health will be. In itself, promoting home cooking might be an
insufficient measure – what matters is not quantity but quality of home cooking. Ambition: The apps and tools of
DietWise are designed such that nutrition guidelines become organically embedded into existing food
environments. Specifically, our solutions efficiently integrate nutrition guidelines into consumer decision-making
and into actions of food environment actors in two ways: the Responsible Cooking Alliance (RCA) initiative will
stimulate influencers to align their actions with national dietary advice, while RecipeWatch will address alignment
with nutrition guidelines from the citizen side by empowering them to ‘correct’ each recipe so that it is healthier
and more sustainable.

Ambition 2: Shifting responsibility for enhancing healthy and sustainable diets toward digital food environment actors

State of the art. Cookbooks shaped our dietary habits; for example, "Ons kookboek" (eng. Our Cookbook), first
issued in 1929, made the Flemish diet less monotonous by advocating three meals a day and an increased usage of
vegetables and animal products [^10]. However, citizens nowadays have shifted from traditional informational
cookbooks to entertainment cookbooks or online websites authored by influencers – celebrity chefs, lifestyle gurus,
retailers recipe websites and social media influencers [^11]. Celebrity chefs actively construct norms about what is
'good' and 'proper' to eat' [^12] and may exert positive and negative influence [^13]. A comprehensive nutritional
assessment of celebrity chefs' recipes shows that celebrity chefs present higher fat, higher saturated fatty acids,
higher sugars, and salt content than what is recommended by national healthy eating guidelines [^14]. Technologies
enabled a range of broader digital cultural phenomena, such as so-called food porn (posting highly appealing food
images on social media), cookbang/mukbang (posting videos cooking/eating large amounts of foods), and diet
hacking (treating the own body like a machine that needs less food than is burned). Different online subcultures
guide consumers in their nutrition-related decisions, but not always for the better. These phenomena shape
consumer decision-making and represent much stronger influence channels compared to classic influence attempts
by governments. Novel and systemic solutions are needed to counteract such influence. Ambition: DietWise
activities will systemically align cultural and commercial practices with nutrition guidelines. We propose to shift
responsibility for enhancing healthy and sustainable diets aligned with national dietary advice from governments
toward food environment actors – who serve as gatekeepers for citizen decision-making. The core idea of our
project is focused on creating a voluntary reporting system (the RCA) assessing and guiding how much each app,
each celebrity chef, each food blog, or each retailer meets national dietary advice. Thus, instead of top-down
directives, we will explore how to use a bottom-up approach and place the initiative to change dietary behavior
directly in the hands of food environment actors. We believe such a shift will help gradually dampen nutritional
noise and organically merge cultural and commercial practices with a healthier and sustainable food consumption
pattern.

Ambition 3. Managing nutritional noise with systemic changes and novel ICT solutions

State of the art. 71 percent of people have "gone digital" in the kitchen, getting their cooking ideas from
Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok and following foodie influencers on social media [^15]. 62% of people look for recipes
by "just Googling it"; the rest are using cooking/recipes/lifestyle websites like allrecipes.com, food blogs, and
social media platforms Pinterest, Facebook, and TikTok [^16] [^17]. Search engines and influencers play an active and
crucial role in shaping citizens’ diet-related decisions. In these digital environments, citizens face large amounts of
contradicting dietary advice coming from influencers, marketers, governments, doctors, and sports coaches. Yet, to
date, there is no solution that can manage this widespread nutritional noise by streamlining it with nutrition
guidelines. Ambition: DietWise seeks to address these challenges by means of disruptive new approaches that
apply existing scientific methods in novel ways. Central in our approach are systemic changes with the aim of
developing solutions that streamline existing tools and applications (instead of developing new solutions) to foster
healthy and sustainable food provision and to make cooking, eating, and treating food at home the most attractive
choice for all stakeholders. Specifically, the Responsible Cooking Alliance (RCA) add-on tool will entail an
independent software that processes online content – by scraping the content of influencers' websites or mobile
apps and automatically assessing the extent of their compliance with the nutrition guidelines, as well as how much
their content promotes sustainability practices in food provision or cooking. This tool will be powered by artificial
intelligence (AI), weak points of the content will be indicated, and tips on recommended changes for influencers
will be provided. Ultimately, if cooking websites/apps become more aligned with nutrition guidelines, they will
gradually get an improved assessment. The RecipeWatch app will be an AI-based app for citizens that corrects the
content of available online recipes following national nutrition guidelines.

Ambition 4. Empowering citizens to change their diets and influence digital food environments

State of the art. In the current food environments, citizens are passive observers and receivers of widespread
nutritional noise targeted at them. Even though some citizens want to play an active role (for example, 'googling'

can be trusted. Furthermore, this disadvantageous situation for citizens is exacerbated by the fact that digital food

Ambition 5: Raising motivation to cook healthy through matching and personalization

State of the art: Tools and applications are crucial in supporting citizens ' informed, healthy, and sustainable
choices. Yet, a significant challenge to address is the fact that the general motivation for healthy and sustainable
food provision and cooking remains low, and citizens' valuation of existing nutrition guidelines is relatively low.
Traditional communication approaches, such as simple nudging with designated logos, have proved to be able to
capture citizens’ attention, yet are not powerful enough when it comes to actually changing the target behavior – as
it is well documented by the "intention-behavior gap" literature [^19]. In addition, existing communication efforts
promoting the uptake of beneficial tools and applications are based on addressing citizens without considering their
preferences (like simply promoting nutrition advice). Such an approach is incomplete and does not consider
diverging needs, especially leaving behind citizens who live with financial and social constraints. Ambition: To
counteract this situation, we will use state of the art behavioral science insights – personalized motivational
matching. Our project will address the lack of healthy and sustainable food provision and cooking with innovative,
tailored behavioral interventions based on motivational matching that will allow us to align nutrition guidelines,
motivation, and personalized consumer needs. Motivational matching refers to the design and distribution of
persuasive messages such that message features (e.g., the themes emphasized) align with the motivational
characteristics of citizens (e.g., salient goals, needs, values). For instance, messages linking health to cooking at
home would best influence citizens with a salient health goal. Recent research shows evidence that targeting and
personalization can be an effective way to change behaviors for good, and the opportunities to personalize increase
as data becomes more accessible [^20].

Ambition 6: Develop a persuasion toolbox aimed at mitigating resistance to nutrition advice

State of the art: Healthy and sustainable advice is more frequently used and accepted by people who already have
positive attitudes and are 'healthy' and 'green' consumers [^21]. Yet, certain groups of citizens demonstrate a general
resistance to health and sustainability messages [^22]. When negative attitudes towards sustainability are present and
strong, simply nudging people to read nutrition guidelines will also prove to be inefficient as people cannot easily
be convinced to change their resistant attitudes (e.g., hoping that they can gently make a change). Thus, any
influence attempt, as an architectural and subtle cue in the environment, will prove to be inefficient if we talk about
changing the views and behaviors of citizens resistant to health and sustainability ideas. Ambition: The mixed
success of existing efforts to make citizens comply with nutrition guidelines might be partly attributed to the fact
that these efforts rely on persuasion tactics that pitch guidelines as the attractive option, mainly by making use of
so-called Alpha strategies. Complementing these strategies, it is our ambition to develop a portfolio of innovative
interventions that do not so much rely on convincing individuals of the attractiveness of cooking at home in line
with nutrition guidelines but use various persuasion techniques that reduce barriers and diminish resistance (i.e.,
Omega strategies), e.g., relabeling of their behaviors, establishing new ingroup norms, brainstorming to remove
barriers.

## 1.2. Methodology

### 1.2.1 Overall methodology, including underpinning concepts, models and assumptions

Citizens are surrounded by constant nutritional noise brought out by unhealthy and unsustainable food
environments. Contradicting dietary advice comes from governments, doctors, and sports coaches; available
scientific evidence is mixed. As a result, there is a general lack of citizens consensus on what is healthy and
sustainable food provision and what are the best ways of eating and treating food at home. Is there any way to
manage this nutritional noise? We believe there is, but we need (1) to link nutrition guidelines with food provision
and cooking at home, (2) understand what drives the actions of (a) citizens and (b) food environment actors, and (3)
integrate scientific knowledge on the effectiveness of behavioral interventions with smart social innovations.

#### A. PREPARATION: UNDERSTANDING (DIGITAL) FOOD ENVIRONMENTS AND CITIZENS NEEDS (WP2)

Research provides a partial picture of factors that drive decisions about food provision and home cooking. For
example, prior work shows that key factors influencing our food choices are (1) economic determinants such as
cost, income, availability, (2) physical determinants such as access, education, skills (e.g. cooking) and time, (3)
social determinants such as culture, family, peers, and meal patterns, (4) psychological determinants such as mood,
stress and guilt, and (5) attitudes, beliefs and knowledge about food [^23] [^24]. A global analysis of cooking worldwide
shows that factors such as gender, age, household size, and employment status moderate the frequency of cooking
at home [^25]. Regarding information sources, citizens nowadays have shifted from traditional informational
cookbooks to entertainment cookbooks authored by influencers – celebrity chefs, lifestyle gurus, retailers recipe
websites, and social media influencers [^26]. Importantly, the decision about what to cook is primarily made in digital
food environments. For instance, an organic search based on keywords is the most popular way consumers find
recipes online, followed by food blogs, websites, and social media platforms such as Pinterest, Facebook, and
TikTok [^27]. While the existing literature has focused on how citizens navigate and make decisions in digital food
environments, only a few studies specifically target the link between digital food environments, cooking at home,
and compliance with nutrition guidelines. This work shows a hyperabundance of existing nutrition apps or blogs;
they are heavily used and relied on, but they do not guarantee the accuracy and appropriateness of nutrition content
to support healthy behavior [^28]. In addition, the digitalization of food environments is amplifying food deserts and
deteriorating the diets of vulnerable citizens because of lower literacy levels and income. Therefore, we will start
our project by deepening our understanding of (i) how online food environments relate to cooking at home skills
and shape the diverse different needs and motivations of citizens, (ii) what drives the motivation of digital food
environment actors (influencers) to align their activities with nutrition guidelines; and (iii) how to address the needs
of vulnerable people.

#### B. DEVELOPMENT: SOCIAL INNOVATIONS DRIVEN BY BEHAVIORAL INTERVENTIONS, TOOLS AND APPLICATIONS (WP3, WP4, WP5)

Digital food environments play a crucial role in shaping citizens' diet-related decisions. Without addressing the
nutritional noise provided by these environments, we cannot efficiently direct citizens to follow healthy and
sustainable food provision, eating and treating food at home. Therefore, the development stage aims to find an
optimal combination of influence techniques (behavioral interventions), beneficial tools, and applications that can
empower citizens to make an informed (healthy and sustainable) choice and, at the same time, streamline the
nutritional noise of digital food environments (e.g., social media content of influencers, online apps, blogs,
retailers) into the direction of nutrition guidelines. We will address this aim at the micro level (motivating citizens
to make better choices) and meso level (managing influencers' activities). Thus, we will start this stage by
developing behavioral interventions that can prepare the ground and potentially boost the motivation of citizens
for uptaking the beneficial tools and applications. We will integrate this micro-level knowledge into beneficial tools
and applications to further enhance the DietWise framework's effectiveness. While developing DietWise
solutions, we will engage in an active process of co-creation and citizen science to account for the needs of
influencers, citizens, and vulnerable people.

B1. Micro level: Behavioral interventions for citizens boosting the uptake of beneficial tools and apps
In general, over the decades, consumer behavior change campaigns have evolved from the idea that increased
knowledge changes attitudes and attitudes change behavior, over increased reliance on negative emotional appeals
and guilt messaging, to, more recently, a growing emphasis on (i) communicating social and moral norms to trigger
behavioral change [^31], and (ii) harnessing novel social influence strategies to address the needs of resistant
consumers [^32], [^33]. Based on these recent trends and insights and building on the state of the art in behavioral science,
marketing, persuasion, and psychology literatures, we will develop and test novel interventions based on norms
and social influence. However, while promising as a positive behavioral change technique, it is essential to realize
that norms can discourage healthy and sustainable behavior or become an important barrier to changing behaviors
for good. For example, stressing social norms may backfire if the majority norm is NOT to follow dietary
guidelines. Then it would likely be disadvantageous to communicate, "Follow the guidelines! People like you do
follow the guidelines." We will focus on innovative norms interventions, leveraging the power of dynamic or
trending norms. These approaches have been proven to be most effective in promoting sustainable and healthrelated behavior, especially when seeding new socially desirable social norms and changing unsustainable,
unhealthy behaviors that are popular among the majority of the population [^34].

Motivation of resistant, supportive and ambivalent citizens. When talking about motivation, we need to
understand the spectrum of attitudes and beliefs. While some people may totally oppose the official nutrition
guidelines in general, others may feel uncomfortable with changing eating habits, and others may be negative about
skipping meat meals. If we lump all these people together, we lose the depth of understanding and the ability to
influence decision-making. One way to address highly resistant citizens, is to tackle ‘false’ food literacy – people
who have a collection of strong inter-related parallel knowledge and attitudes of what consists of a good diet
(e.g., influencer Andrew Tate, who eats only meat, advises millions of his fans “You need to eat more meat, eat like
an animal”). Combining insights from psychology and marketing literatures, we will aim to reduce such negative
aspects as a norm to eat meat (e.g., ‘eat 500 g meat per day to get enough protein’) or strong preferences formed by
social peer pressure (‘real men eat meat’). Furthermore, we will devote significant attention to gaining insights into
the attitudes and motives of citizens with ambivalent attitudes towards nutrition guidelines – those who are
‘sitting on the fence’ – people who at the same time support healthier and more sustainable diets but still have not
done the shift to change their behaviors. We argue that these people are the ones that should be seen as a particular
target group as they are the most susceptible to any health- and sustainability-related information messages
and may more easily shift to the proponents’ side.

The analysis will focus on (a) existing norms and social influence-based behavioral interventions of proven
effectiveness (i.e., high effect sizes) that can be applied in cooking advice provision contexts; (b) identification of
new interventions; (c) special focus on Omega influence techniques that reduce resistance in addition to a proven
‘toolbox’ of Alpha strategies. For example, one of the most effective Omega strategies for resistance is not to raise
it in the first place. The message can be redefined not as an attempt to convince someone with arguments about the
benefits of the behavior, but framed as a cooperative endeavor to arrive at beneficial outcomes for public health [^35].
Another Omega strategy to indirectly disable resistance is asking to cast people into specific roles – an influencer
can disable a target’s resistance by asking to take the role of “health expert” [^36].

Once the most efficient basic interventions are known, we will test how their effectiveness depends on nationality,
religion, culture, regionality, and seasonality and what kind of behavioral interventions are most efficient in
promoting better choices linked with waste reduction and delivery. We will focus on identifying boosting vs.
suppressing effects and devote special attention to understanding conditions when interventions can backfire. For
instance, certain modern social norms may go against religious practices or culture. On the other side, identifying
when interventions are most efficient provides an unexplored potential to identify targeted and powerful
mechanisms leading to effective adoption of tools and applications. We will formulate a matrix tabulating the
effectiveness of interventions for different groups of factors. Finally, we will ensure that behavioral interventions
are adequately adapted to vulnerable groups’ needs and evaluate accessibility for vulnerable people using
the 8Bs methodology developed by VIGL. Specifically, we will assess different components of accessibility for
vulnerable groups: affordability, usability, reliability, comprehensibility, understanding, availability, and familiarity
(see T3.4).

The central underlying logic to boost the motivation will be based on motivational matching that will allow us to
align motivation and personalized consumer needs with nutrition guidelines. Motivational matching refers to the
design and distribution of persuasive messages such that message features (e.g., the themes emphasized) align with
[^37] the motivational characteristics of citizens (e.g., salient goals, needs, values). For instance, messages linking
health to cooking at home would best influence citizens with a salient health goal. Recent research, indeed, shows
evidence that targeting and personalization can be an effective way to change behaviors for good, and the
opportunities to personalize increase as data become more accessible [^38].

Experimentation will be the primary research method to account for our results because experimental designs are
the major method for making causal inferences [^39]. All interventions will first be tested in the lab/online settings, and
the effectiveness of interventions will be assessed based on the replicability of effects, effect sizes, and statistical
significance. Interventions that surpass our predetermined statistical effectiveness thresholds will be further tested
in pilot settings.

B2. Meso level: Integrating scientific knowledge on effectiveness of behavioral interventions with smart digital and social innovations

Systemic solutions are needed to streamline the nutritional noise provided by digital food environments. A way to
handle food environments is to shift responsibility for enhancing healthy and sustainable diets aligned with national
dietary advice from governments toward food environment actors – who serve as gatekeepers for citizen decisionmaking. Our project's core idea is to create a voluntary reporting initiative that is based on the principles of selfregulation – the Responsible Cooking Alliance (RCA). This initiative will help and guide influencers (i.e. app
developers, celebrity chefs, food bloggers, retailers) to assess to what extent their advice meets national dietary
advice. Thus, instead of top-down directives, we will explore how to use a bottom-up approach and place the
initiative to change dietary behavior directly in the hands of food environment actors. A self-evaluation system will
be created that will set standards and allow evaluation of how much activities of influencers are in line with
nutrition guidelines (e.g., do they promote healthier and more sustainable choices, provide weekly meal planning,
promote efforts to reduce waste, help to make delivery choices, address accessibility to vulnerable groups). The
more compliant with the nutritional guidelines influencers are, the higher the rating they will receive. The
influencers will be able to join this initiative voluntarily, as well as choose the extent to which to participate in it.
Thus, the RCA will work similarly to the world's most widely used sustainability reporting initiative, the Global
Reporting Initiative, which provides a number of standards to report, which also serve as a roadmap to enhance
sustainability performance; or the initiative Jonvabaliai (eng. Fireflies) aiming to improve the public perception of
transparency in the use of EU funds by providing a platform and voluntary self-evaluation and reporting tool. The
criteria for the evaluation will be created using co-creation processes with national governments, nutrition
scientists, influencers, and citizens. The influencers will be motivated to join this initiative for several reasons. This
initiative will be created from the bottom up, meaning that the demand and pressure to implement positive changes
will come from consumers – the industry's clients. The initiative will serve as a step toward self-regulation,
benefiting both food environment stakeholders and citizens (see The International Council for Advertising SelfRegulation as a successful case for self-regulation ). Influencers will be able to take control of their actions
themselves without regulatory pressure from governments. Further, the more reporting standards are fulfilled, the
higher the rating for apps and blogs will be provided. Thus, influencers can exploit their efforts to strengthen their
reputation and gain a competitive advantage – becoming more trustworthy for consumers and attractive to
advertising clients. The initiative will support them with certification and marketing materials, and will disseminate
information about them as good exemplars.

##### Responsible Cooking Alliance (RCA) add-on tool – ICT solutions

The RCA will be supported with state of the art ICT solutions that will address the needs of two different
stakeholder groups: (1) influencers representing gatekeepers for nutrition-related decision-making and (2) citizens.
We will develop an add-on tool for influencers that will automatically scrape the content of their websites or
mobile apps and automatically assess the extent of their compliance with the nutrition guidelines, as well as how
much their content promotes sustainability practices in food provision or cooking. Influencers will integrate this
RCA add-on tool into their existing software systems, i.e., websites or apps. We will employ Javascript
technologies and the Angular language for building dynamic and interactive user interfaces to ensure that the addon is compatible and can be smoothly integrated into existing systems, we will support influencers by integrating
the add-on tool too. This tool will be powered by artificial intelligence (AI), meaning that weak points of the
content will be indicated, and tips on recommended changes for influencers will be provided. Ultimately, if
cooking websites/apps become more aligned with nutrition guidelines, they will gradually get a higher score. The
RCA add-on tool will provide features that enrich the content of cooking websites/apps. For instance, influencers
will be able to integrate and offer their followers additional functionalities, e.g., a weekly meal planning tool with
personalized guidance for a better menu, a system for rating recipes according to various criteria (e.g., how much
waste is left after preparing the meal, what was the feeling after eating this meal, etc.), guidance on healthier eating
and waste reduction, sustainable delivery options, etc. Such functionalities will not only provide a better experience
for their users but also make a positive impact on their RCA score and reputation, meaning more traffic to
websites/apps and, thus, financial benefits to their business. Features will be defined and tested during co-creation
processes.

##### RecipeWatch app

The RecipeWatch app will be an AI-based app for citizens that corrects the content of available online recipes
following national nutrition guidelines. This app will ensure that even if the influencer’s website, blog, etc., is not a
part of RCA, the content of it will not stay in a grey zone, as citizens might use the RecipeWatch to get support on
how to make a better decision regarding healthier and more sustainable nutrition. The app will work similarly to
popular AI-powered tools for writing and spelling errors (e.g., Grammarly or Ludwig). Yet, the RecipeWatch will
not test for grammar but will test for alignment with nutrition guidelines, i.e., it will scan the content of the recipe
the user is reading and make suggestions in real time. If the recipe lists ingredients that are not so healthy or
sustainable, citizens will see alternative options. For instance, if a recipe suggests using palm oil, RecipeWatch will
suggest a correction and provide healthier and more sustainable options. The user will be able to “accept” or
“reject” suggestions. Citizens will be invited to co-create the replacement options and rate the altered recipe after
cooking it. As such, the app not only will improve recipes in terms of health and sustainability, but maintain or
even improve the taste vis-a-vis the original recipe. In getting/accepting/rejecting/adopting/rating the correction,
citizens will help the RecipeWatch to learn and also shape the food environment by providing better advice for
others. If some recipes are very robust to the specifics of ingredients or the amounts, the app will advise to choose
healthier or more sustainable alternative ingredients (e.g., switch to organic ingredients, reduce the salt, the sugar or
use ¾ of the fat that is normally used). The RecipeWatch will empower citizens by helping them to gain knowledge
about what is unhealthy or unsustainable in the recipe composition and give them the skills to correct it. Further, if
a user agrees to register (e.g., using Google or other accounts), RecipeWatch will offer personalized guidance
(based on motivational matching) and recommendations based on user profiles (e.g., diet type, allergies, religion,
meal budget, etc.). Developing this app, we will build on the best practices and know-how of our partner IHU, who
was part of the project Protein, which successfully implemented a personalized nutrition guide. Additionally, we
will create a browser extension tool of RecipeWatch –citizens can use the extension when browsing for recipes
online and via search engines. Citizens will have to download, install, and sign into the extension, and it will start
guidance across all recipe websites citizens visit. To correspond to the citizens’ needs, specific features will be
defined and tested during co-creation processes (WP4). The main innovation of this AI tool is its dynamicity to
user needs, i.e., its ability to automatically adjust to a user’s nutritional profile while requiring individuals to
declare factors that make a recipe for them more attractive.

B3. Innovative ICT solutions

(1) The Responsible Cooking Alliance (RCA) add-on tool will automatically scrape the content of influencers’
websites/mobile apps and provide a score representing their compliance with the national nutrition guidelines and
promotion of sustainability practices. Specific criteria for creating the score will be defined during co-creation
processes (T4.1). We will use technologies like Scrapy, Puppeteer, Beautiful Soup, Python Requests for content
scraping. Further, we will leverage the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the Agile software
development approach and will use AI and NLP tools to check if the content shared by influencers adheres to
national dietary and sustainability advice. Additionally, it will offer automated recommendations for influencers on
how to enhance content quality, compliance, and user engagement. We will develop a full-fledged and AI-powered
software that extends the functionality of an existing website/mobile app and allows customization. These
functionalities will be easily integrated into existing influencers’ and retailers’ ICT systems (guaranteeing
interoperability and configurations) and will cater to different local languages and web and mobile app
development platforms, content management systems. We will meticulously tailor digital tools with prominent
Content Management Platforms (CMPs) like WordPress, Wix, Shopify, and Squarespace, considering the distinct
architecture and Application Programming Interface (API) of each.

(2) The RecipeWatch app will serve as AI-powered software extending the functionality of existing cooking
recipe websites, social media profiles, and apps. Personalized guidance relevant criteria will be defined during cocreation processes in WP4. Personalization will be provided implicitly by the contemporary learning strategies
embedded in the AI-empowered tools. In particular, continual learning will be provided along with Few-Shot
Learning and Relevance Feedback. The goal of these learning schemes is to automatically update the parameters of
the AI model to fit the current user needs, as these preferences are implicitly indicated to the AI tool during user
interaction with the system. In other words, the user approves or rejects the corrective suggestion during the
interaction with the system, and the system automatically updates its response so that the following retrieval results
in a better fit with the user profile than the current ones. More specifically, upon a user selection on a nutrition
profile of interest or not, the AI mechanisms try to identify the common patterns that have led the user to accept
some suggestions while rejecting others. This is due to the taste of food and how this fits our own needs but also to
other diet restrictions like minimal usage of salt or sugar, fasting, price of the product, allergies, etc.

B4. Engagement through co-creation to boost innovations

To boost the effectiveness of digital and social innovations, we will engage citizens and relevant stakeholders in cocreation to develop new approaches to innovation and validate apps, tools, and personalized behavioral interventions. We will develop new approaches to innovation by influencers, app developers, and retailers directly
and through various associations across Europe (e.g., DOT Europe, the World Influencers and Bloggers
Association, European Influencers Academy). We will ensure full alignment with national nutritional
guidelines and consumer-centricity of applications and tools, and personalized behavioral interventions by
engaging citizens, consumer organizations, and institutions working with developing nutrition guidelines in
Belgium, Greece, and Lithuania (e.g., VIGL, VU, PHB, IHU, PROL). The relevant stakeholders will be involved at
all levels of developing our solutions – from the joint generation of initial ideas about the attractiveness of various
interventions to prototyping, testing, and reviewing developed apps and tools. As to the citizens, we will ensure that
our proposed solutions address the diverse needs of citizens (e.g., resistant, vulnerable, and supporting citizens).
The co-creation approach will ensure faster and broader involvement of expertise and help connect project results
with real-world context. The co-creation will start by exploring the interests and needs of the citizens to see if a
mutually acceptable basis and agreement on the acceptance of nutrition guidelines can be established. Citizens and
stakeholders will be involved in sharing their ideas and knowledge about food provision, cooking at home, and
discussing various relevant issues (e.g., how to change behaviors for the better, how to avoid the negative influence
of regulatory paternalism in communicating nutrition guidelines). We will organize a co-creation workshop for
diverse stakeholder groups in every pilot country (3 workshops per pilot). During the workshops, we aim to
determine (i) the criteria, mechanism, and processes for the voluntary reporting initiative, (ii) the preferred
functionalities for the initiative, (iii) various extents of possible stakeholders' participation levels, and (iv) the
expected benefits from the initiative. Co-creation provides insights into the needs of stakeholder groups and creates
a sense of ownership over the RCA initiative, thus increasing the positive impact on future collaboration and
participation.

In close collaboration with selected stakeholder groups in each pilot country, we will also co-develop the best ICT
solutions. We will engage stakeholders in ICT solutions co-developing processes, such as testing and refining the
prototypes of proposed ICTs. To ensure that the proposed ICTs fulfill the requirements and needs of each
stakeholder group, we will gather three different groups of test users representing influencers, citizens, and
vulnerable citizens in each pilot country. Together with each stakeholder group, we will perform exploratory
prototype tests and usability tests combining qualitative and quantitative methods, such as in-person and moderated
user tests for qualitative commentary, followed by unmoderated remote tests for quantitative feedback. To ensure
standardization, a clear dedicated plan for the test and key metrics to track will be identified for each group. Codevelopment will deliver detailed guidelines for the final development of ICT solutions that enable highly functional and user-centric tools, fostering improved engagement and aligning with the unique dynamics of each
stakeholder group.

B5. Digital and social innovations powered by citizen science

Extant research indicates that new technologies, social media, and mobile apps may serve as main platforms and
vehicles when considering how to change consumers' attitudes. We will develop the RCA initiative and the
RecipeWatch app with an aim to train citizens' skills in recognizing the importance of aligning behaviors with
nutrition guidelines. Our tool and apps will serve as a platform of social innovation that changes the food

C. PILOT TESTING: VALIDATING APPS, TOOLS AND INTERVENTIONS IN REAL LIFE SETTINGS (WP7)

Following the development stage, we will move to pilot testing and validation. Pilot testing aims to deploy and
operate the DietWise methodologies and interventions in the pilot countries. We will assess the effectiveness of
behavioral interventions in conjunction with the RCA add-on and RecipeWatch, taking into account factors such as
culture, religion, seasonality, and regionality. In addition, the pilot testing stage will validate the impact of digital
tools (RCA and RecipeWatch) and engage citizens in citizen science activities.

We will start by developing the pilot deployment methodology tailored to each pilot country's local needs,
challenges, and national nutritional policies. We will integrate this knowledge with theory, practice, and empirical
evidence. Using problem-solving techniques, we will compile the blueprint for designing our interventions to be
tested in the development stage. Our researchers will work closely with pilot partners to define the most promising
approaches and interventions and delineate possible constraints and adaptations of existing interventions, apps, and
tools. Together, we will co-select interventions tailored to the specific needs of the pilot countries. In the pilot
deployment stage, we will evaluate the effectiveness of behavioral interventions in conjunction with tools and
applications and factors such as culture, religion, seasonality, and regionality. As a result, in one country, we
may concentrate on fine-tuning social norms-based interventions; in another – on developing a set of innovative,
high-risk, and high-gain interventions promoting home cooking and healthy meals for resistant citizens; in another
– promotional behavioral interventions for vulnerable people. Next, we will assess the cost efficiency of each
intervention, as well as its scalability and ease of implementation. Finally, we will explore how well interventions
fit our pilot contexts, fit with the existing apps and tools in the pilots, and reflect the needs of crucial populations
and contexts.

Each pilot country has specific needs and challenges to address. The digital food environment in Belgium and
Greece features a considerable number of influencers already encouraging followers to choose healthier recipes and
follow national nutritional guidelines, at least to a partial degree. Therefore, it will be easier to implement
additional improvements in these countries, such as providing advice on food sustainability and offering weekly
meal planning tools. However, in Lithuania, most influencers do not adhere to national nutritional guidelines or
provide healthier recipes, sustainability information, or advice on weekly meal planning. Therefore, more effort
will be required to promote a significant shift.

Across all pilot countries, we will focus our main attention on launching the RCA initiative and the RecipeWatch
app – as well as on integrating the most efficient behavioral interventions in these activities. Building on the
knowledge gained in WP2-WP5, the Belgian pilot will encourage influencers who participated in co-creation
activities (WP4) to voluntarily align their meal recipes with nutritional guidelines via participation in RCA. To
broaden our scope, we will also target additional actors, both macro-influencers (e.g., Hot Regressive cuisine,
Sergio Herman, Sandra Bekkari) and micro-influencers (e.g., Laura Van den Broeck; Helen Verhelst). We will also
provide guidance and individual consultations on how to integrate beneficial tools and apps into influencers'
activities. Next, we will implement our behavioral interventions using ZekerGezond.be and websites/apps by
celebrity chefs, nutritionists, influencers, and retailers. In addition, we will focus on retail industry representatives –
Foodbag.be offering meal kits to consumers. Recent evidence shows that meal kit delivery has health-promoting
potential, specifically in increasing family vegetable intake [^40]. We will consider integrating ZekerGezond recipes
into the Foodbag.be webpage and app to increase the supply and demand for healthier meal kits. Also, we will test
different selected interventions in Foodbag.be web pages and/or apps and measure their effectiveness in boosting
citizens ' motivation and increasing healthier food purchases.
Regarding addressing vulnerable citizens' needs, the Belgian pilot focuses on the needs of vulnerable children and
adolescents (e.g., youth on aid). We will share knowledge with vulnerable groups on the potential of the RCA and
RecipeWatch, explain the benefits of linking decisions to nutrition guidelines, and encourage them to use our tools
and apps. Next, we will test what additional features are needed for vulnerable children and adolescents to use
existing digital tools for healthier meals at home (e.g., ensuring easy and fast recipes, testing affordable mailbox
delivery systems with recipe ingredients).
In Greece the digital food environment is dominated by celebrity chefs (e.g., the most influential one is Akis
Petretzikis) and bloggers (e.g., Paxxi; Madame Ginger). First, we will use the same approach as in Belgium and

addressing vulnerable citizens' needs, the Greek pilot will focus on families in low SES areas. We will develop a
nudging package targeting teachers' and students' families in collaboration with a multidisciplinary team of
nutritionists/dietitians, health promotion specialists, school nurses, and influencers. This package will also include

competitions with healthy homemade food made using RecipeWatch and cooking workshops using leftovers to
prevent food waste. Workshops will engage the entire school community. A "school health promotion" group with
representatives from the school community, education and health fields, public health authorities, and parent
associations will be created to support efficient interactions.

Integrating findings

Testing our interventions in real-life settings in pilot countries will enable us to integrate findings from the
Preparation, Development, and Pilot testing stages. Specifically, we will connect the valuable knowledge and
insights about existing nutrition guideline promotion approaches, factors driving motivation to seek healthy
and sustainable food provision and cooking at home, with a selected set of ICT solutions and accompanied
behavioral interventions for each pilot country. Using the AI-based apps RCA and RecipeWatch we will
analyze usage data and will provide information about how to fine-tune our approach and actions (e.g., behavioral
incentives, influencer engagement) to raise their effectiveness. Integrating findings will drive the fine-tuning of the
DietWise approach and provide valuable insights for strengthening its adaptability and replication potential.

To assess the results and the effectiveness of our overarching framework, interventions, and tools, we will use
the Intervention Mapping protocol [^41]. We will combine and integrate statistical criteria with the planning,
management, and efficiency evaluation. Based on statistical criteria, we will test for effect sizes, mediation, and
moderation. After selecting the most statistically powerful interventions, we will screen them for usefulness (for
example, interventions with rebound effects for a large group of people can be less beneficial than interventions
without such effects but with smaller effect sizes), the extent to which they are potentially sustainable (for
example, interventions can have a large effect size but can be non-sustainable in some contexts).
For all studies, we will perform a power analysis using G*Power [^42] to determine the sample size. We will define
the magnitude effect sizes based on the effect size reported in the available prior research, meta-analysis studies,
or our own data.

D. BUILDING CAPABILITIES AND TRUST IN NUTRITION GUIDELINES (WP8)

Following the deployment of pilots and testing of interventions, apps, and tools, we will strengthen influencers'
capabilities, develop integrity guidelines, and increase general trust in nutrition guidelines. Citizens do not follow
the nutrition guidelines because the guidelines are not consistently featured in dietary advice/influencer actions. A
deeper understanding of the importance of nutrition guidelines and ethical responsibility for driving consumer
decisions among influencers and retailers represents one of the biggest challenges in dealing with trust in nutrition
guidelines. To help address this challenge, we will engage and ensure that stakeholders have the needed knowledge
and capacity to align their activities with nutrition guidelines in line with integrity principles. First, we will develop
a better understanding of integrity aspects related to food provision and cooking advice – we will define the
boundaries for influencers and companies to guide consumers in their decision-making: how to provide cooking
advice in a responsible manner, where are the limits for promoting unhealthy recipes, is ethical to capitalize on
vulnerabilities of citizens. Second, we will develop a dedicated training program on integrity principles and
voluntary reporting principles (RCA initiative). Third, we will ensure that citizens are well-informed and
empowered on how to make informed, healthy, and sustainable food choices. Together with SAFE, we will develop
consumer empowerment guidelines to ensure they can make informed choices aligned with nutrition guidelines.

1.2.2. Technical robustness of the developed AI tools and algorithms

DietWise will ensure the technical robustness of AI tools by adhering to ethical and legal standards, emphasizing
privacy preservation, liability considerations, and robust AI. For Ethical AI, factors such as data origin, processing
integrity, and aggregation procedures will be addressed in the DietWise design of the tools, incorporating biascleaning algorithms. Lawful AI implementation will comply with GDPR featuring a designated Data Protection
Officer (DPO) and adherence to Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA). The Ethical/Data Manager (ETDM)
will mitigate risks and ensure compliance with local/national ethical approvals. Personal data access and
aggregation will follow GDPR consent procedures, and user-controlled data management tools will enhance control
and transparency. The AI-based tools and apps will incorporate terms and conditions for privacy and information
sharing. Liability considerations will involve risk assessment of ethical AI decisions, while robustness
will ensure consistent performance amid uncertainties, building confidence throughout the AI lifecycle (see 1.2.8).

1.2.6. Gender related aspects
The project aims to incorporate gender aspects into every stage of the research and innovation cycle, in order to
contribute to the achievement of the UN Sustainable Development Goal 5 – “Achieve gender equality and
empower all women and girls” – and the EU's Gender Equality Strategy 2020-2025. The project follows the
principles of gender equality, from ensuring a gender balance in the team and decision-making process to
integrating gender dimensions into the research and innovation content. This will help to improve the scientific
quality and societal relevance of the knowledge, technology, and innovation produced. Furthermore, the DietWise
project will use an inclusive approach to understand gender inequalities via intersectionality theory. This approach
ensures that gender is not considered in isolation but is instead viewed in relation to other forms of discrimination,
such as ethnicity, sexual orientation, age, and gender identity. By adopting an intersectional approach, the project’s
team can control for possible bias and aim for excellence in research and technology, or innovation produced.
Gender plays a crucial role in the context of food provision and cooking at home. Not only are there gender
differences related to the amount and type of healthy and sustainable consumption, but gender stereotypes and
norms also impact the way people think about this issue, respond to it, and make decisions to act 45. Gender analysis
is important in different aspects of this project. In general, women tend to have more positive attitudes towards
healthy eating than men, which greatly influences their dietary behavior 46.However, research suggests that women
tend to focus on the fat content of food while deciding on healthy food, which may lead to malnutrition as they
overlook other important nutrients 47. The project will examine how gender differences affect attitudes and
behaviors toward sustainable and healthy diets. By identifying these differences, we can find ways to break down
gender barriers and increase motivation to eat sustainably and healthily. The DietWise will investigate the impact
of various approaches to address general society, vulnerable, and resistant citizens and tailor the interventions to
meet their needs, which will contribute to building a respectful, healthier, and more sustainable food environment
that promotes diversity. Thus, the research part of the project will be conducted from a gender perspective to
personalize the project results based on this criterion. The project aims to ensure the active participation of women
and the LGBTIQ+ community, who are often disproportionately vulnerable in unsustainable and unhealthy food
environments, by following an inclusive approach and tailoring the interventions to the individual needs of
vulnerable citizens. Gender aspects will be taken into account during the implementation phase by (1) ensuring
equal distribution of male and female participants in all activities, especially those engaging citizens directly (e.g.,
surveys, focus groups, co-creation), (2) performing separate statistical analysis across gender dimensions to
uncover any gender differences or biases, taking into account other factors intersecting with gender, such as
race/ethnicity, age, gender identity, and sexual orientation, (3) analyzing all collected results (e.g., qualitative,
quantitative) through gender lenses to provide high-quality insights, and (4) using fully inclusive communication
techniques and project dissemination activities, including adopting gender and diversity-sensitive language and
aiming at gender and diversity balanced participation.

DietWise's work plan is structured in ten interrelated work packages for 36 months. WP1 and WP6 aim to ensure
smooth planning and collaboration between the project partners, meeting the highest quality standards and
mitigating risks. The WP2 will map the current state of behavioral aspects related to nutrition guidelines uptake,
enhancement of healthy and sustainable food provision, eating and treating food at home. This will be achieved by
providing institutional-level insights about key interventions to promote beneficial tools and applications uptake, a
large EU-wide survey for understanding the needs, barriers, and drivers of citizens, analysis on how to boost the
motivation of influencers, and identifying the needs, barriers, and drivers of vulnerable citizens. Next, in WP3, we
will develop a package of the most efficient behavioral interventions. This will be achieved by testing two large
groups of interventions (norms and social influence). These interventions will be linked to factors such as
nationality, religion, culture, regionality, and seasonality, reducing waste and delivery choices and matching them
with the needs of vulnerable citizens. In the WP4, we will validate our activities through co-creation. Specifically,
using co-creation with stakeholders and citizens, we will arrive at the final set of interventions, fine-tune the
mechanism of the Responsible Cooking Alliance, and co-develop the AI-based app RecipeWatch. Simultaneously,
we will create a corresponding capacity-building curriculum. WP4 will result in the development of the overall
framework of DietWise. In WP5, we will provide tailored action plans per pilot country and guarantee proper
stakeholder engagement and ICT solutions ready for the pilots' launch. The objective of WP7 is to deploy and
operate the DietWise framework, methodologies, and interventions in the pilot countries. We will assess the
effectiveness of different tools and applications in combination with the most efficient behavioral interventions and
factors such as culture, religion, seasonality, regionality. In addition, WP7 will validate the impact of digital tools
and engage citizens in citizen science activities. WP8 will ensure that the expected outcomes of DietWise are
relevant and suitable for use in other EU countries, strengthen the capabilities of stakeholders, and increase
knowledge on integrity principles and cooking at home aligned with nutrition guidelines. This will be achieved by
developing policy recommendations, integrity standards for the industry, training influencers, and setting
guidelines for consumers. Finally, both WP9 and WP10 will encompass horizontal activities to ensure high
visibility of DietWise's expected outcomes, establish synergies with other EU initiatives, and ensure the long-term
sustainability of project activities.WP9 and WP10 will ensure that the project results are relevant and acceptable for
application in other EU countries, the project results will be widely supported, and stakeholders will be motivated
to apply knowledge created by DietWise.
